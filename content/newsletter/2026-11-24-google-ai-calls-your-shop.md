## The big one

_AI is now on both sides of the counter: your customers' assistants are calling you, and your own tools are getting access to your money._

### Who is on the other end of the line?
<!-- published: 2026-11-16 -->

On November 16, Google said its Ask Maps feature can now [find beauty, pet care, auto and home repair appointments online and call local businesses](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-holiday-travel-features-2026/) on a customer's behalf. It starts in the US. If you take bookings by phone, some of those calls will soon come from Google, not from the person who wants the appointment. Google's help page on its automated calls to businesses says they are [monitored and recorded for quality assurance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7690269).

A day later, Google made [Tasks in Gemini free](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/) in the US and five other countries. It runs errands across the accounts you connect, like email, calendar and payment cards. Google's own example: it called two car dealerships at once to book a service. It's designed to ask before it clicks "Buy", and you can set a hard spending cap. And in the coming weeks, Google says, people will be able to [link their Angi and Thumbtack accounts in Search](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/connected-apps-holiday-errands/) to find local professionals. If you're in the trades, those profiles are about to matter more.

Now the other side. When you connect an AI tool to your own accounts, several big banks have decided who answers for it. On November 18, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/) that Citi and PNC had joined other big banks in updating their terms. Citi's [updated terms](https://www.citi.com/tandc) say it "will consider any access and actions taken by the agent as authorized by You", even if the login you gave the agent leaks, and they cover sole proprietors and small business users. Wells Fargo's [new online access agreement](https://www.wellsfargo.com/online-banking/online-access-agreement/upcoming/), which covers Business Online and takes effect November 24, says that once you let an AI agent in, its actions are "deemed to have been initiated and authorized directly by you, without further verification." PNC's [updated agreement](https://www.pnc.com/content/dam/digital-assets/en/documents/digital_services_agreement_UPDATE.pdf), effective December 19, tells you not to hand an agent your user ID, password or one-time code at all.

Put those together and you have two jobs. Make your business easy for other people's AI to find and book correctly. And keep your own AI on a leash, because if it moves money by mistake, the bank's position is that you did it.

> **What to do this week**
> - In your Google Business Profile, go to Business Profile settings, then Advanced settings, then "Google automated calls and text messages", and decide whether to keep "Bookings and inquiries from customers" on. Google hasn't said it covers Ask Maps calls.
> - Tell whoever answers your phone that a call from Google about a booking is a real customer, and check that your hours, prices and services are current everywhere an assistant might read them.
> - Never give an AI tool your bank login or a one-time code. List the apps connected to your bank and accounting software, remove the ones you don't use, and write down the date you did it.

## Quick hits

#### Tools you use

<!-- published: 2026-11-19 -->
**Mailchimp is being sold.** Intuit agreed to [sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1331/intuit-reports-first-quarter-results-and-enters-agreement-to-sell-mailchimp-to-bending-spoons) for about $3.68 billion. The deal is expected to close in early 2027, pending regulatory approval, and the announcement says nothing about pricing or product changes. **For you:** nothing changes yet. Export your contact list and templates now and note your renewal date, so any decision later is yours.

<!-- published: 2026-11-19 -->
**Gemini can now drive your Mac.** Google's [Gemini Mac app can click, type and fill in forms](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/computer-use-mac/) across your apps. It needs a Mac with Apple silicon on macOS 15 or newer, a personal Google account and a paid Google AI plan, US only for now, and you choose which apps it can touch. **For you:** try it on one boring chore, and keep your banking and accounting apps off its list. PNC's new terms forbid letting an AI agent click around its site for you.

<!-- published: 2026-11-16 -->
**Two small Google Workspace changes.** Calendar's "Speedy meetings" is becoming a [buffer that ends meetings 5 minutes early](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/schedule-meetings-with-default-buffer-google-calendar.html). Docs can now [compare two Word files](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/review-edits-across-microsoft-word-docx-and-Google-Docs-files.html) without converting them. **For you:** meetings ending early? Check Calendar's event duration setting.

#### Money

<!-- published: 2026-11-19 -->
**Stripe lets AI agents move money, with a human in the loop.** For businesses on Stripe Treasury, Stripe opened a preview where [AI agents can pay vendors and move funds within limits you set](https://stripe.com/blog/give-your-agents-controlled-access-to-analyze-your-data-and-move-funds). By default, refunds, payouts and transfers an agent starts need a person's approval. **For you:** if you ever connect an AI tool to your Stripe account, start with read-only access and leave those approvals on.

<!-- published: 2026-11-16 -->
**The seat price is not the bill.** Microsoft paid for a study, with Microsoft running the tests and the research firm GigaOm validating the method and results, that priced a modeled everyday user's month, at list prices, at [$30 on Copilot, $81 on ChatGPT Enterprise and $205 on Claude Enterprise](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/2026/11/16/what-ai-at-work-really-costs/) once usage charges were added to the subscriptions. Microsoft sells one of the three, so read it as a reminder, not a ranking. **For you:** open your last AI invoice and look for usage or overage lines on top of the subscription.

## Repo of the week: TaxHacker

TaxHacker is a free, open-source app that turns photos of receipts, invoices and PDFs into a table of dates, amounts, merchants and taxes, then exports it to CSV with the documents attached for your accountant. You pick the AI that reads them: OpenAI, Gemini, Mistral, or one running on your own computer.

**Setup:** medium. It runs on Docker (a standard way to install server software) on a computer that stays on. If that means nothing to you, hand the link to whoever does your IT. **Cost:** the app is free; you pay your AI provider for what it reads, or nothing if you run a model on your own machine. The author calls it early development ("use at your own risk"), so keep your current bookkeeping running alongside it. We read the docs, haven't run it. ## Prompt of the week: chase an unpaid invoice

When your first reminder got no reply and you still want the customer.

```
I run a small business and a customer hasn't paid an invoice. Help me get paid without damaging the relationship.

Details:
- Customer: [business name] (contact: [first name])
- Invoice: [number], [amount], for [what the work was]
- Due date: [date]
- What I've already sent: [e.g. one friendly reminder on (date), no reply]
- How much I want to keep this customer: [a lot / some / not much, and why]

Write a short follow-up email (under 120 words) that:
1. Puts the invoice number, amount and due date in the first two lines.
2. Assumes it was missed, not ignored, but asks for a specific payment date.
3. Gives one easy way to pay, and one easy way to tell me if something on the invoice is wrong.
4. Sounds like a person, not a collections agency. No threats, no "per my last email".

Then give me one line on what to do if there's still no reply in 7 days.
```

What one run with made-up details gave back:

> Following up on invoice #1042 for $1,850, the October window repairs, which was due October 31. I'm guessing it slipped through the cracks, which happens to everyone.
>
> Could you let me know what date you'll be able to pay it? Even a rough day works.
>
> If anything on the invoice looks off, just reply with "question" and a line about what, and I'll sort it out right away.
